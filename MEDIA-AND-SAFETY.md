# Media and Safety Policy

How photos and video are received, vetted, displayed, and how the project protects itself
from illegal or harmful uploads. Read before enabling anything that accepts files.

This is operational guidance, not legal advice. The legal points below are flagged so you
can get proper advice where it matters.

---

## 1. The core principle

Do NOT put an open, anonymous file-upload box on your own website. Two reasons:

1. An anonymous upload box is an abuse magnet. It is exactly what bad actors look for to
   dump illegal material, and you would be receiving and hosting it blind.
2. Under the UK Online Safety Act, illegal-content duties came into force in March 2025
   and apply to any service that lets users upload or share content. Ofcom has named
   file-upload and file-sharing services as a top enforcement priority. Hosting public
   uploads can pull your own site into scope as a regulated user-to-user service, with
   risk-assessment and content-removal duties attached. Not hosting uploads keeps you
   out of the heaviest part of that regime.

So: media is gated, never open. It arrives through a controlled, human path, not a public
upload form.

## 2. How media comes in (gated, two-stage)

Stage one, the public form (already built): text plus an optional "evidence links" field
plus a simple flag, "I have photo or video evidence (yes/no)". No file upload. If someone
has media, they paste a link to their own hosting (their cloud drive, an image host,
video platform) or just tick the flag.

Stage two, human follow-up: if a report looks credible and the person flagged media, you
contact them through the opt-in email they gave and arrange transfer for that specific,
vetted case. Media therefore only ever arrives from a corresponding, semi-identified
person about a report you have already looked at. That single step removes the anonymous
blind-upload attack surface almost entirely, and it improves the evidence too, because you
get provenance and a person to ask follow-up questions.

If you ever want smoother capture later, the safer managed option is a sign-in-required
upload (for example a Google Form that stores to your own Drive), which adds a light
accountability layer and keeps files in your control. Only consider that once you have a
written process for the risks below. Never a fully anonymous public upload.

## 3. Vetting media

Before anything is published:
- Provenance: who took it, when, where, on what. Does it match the account?
- Metadata (EXIF): check it for verification. GPS and timestamp can confirm or contradict
  the claimed location and time. Treat a match as supporting, not proof.
- Reverse image search: check the image is not lifted from elsewhere online.
- Plausibility: obvious lens flares, birds, aircraft, known hoaxes, and AI generation.
- Score it on the same Evidence axis as everything else. Weak media can still be logged,
  labelled as weak.

## 4. Displaying media

- Re-host it yourself after review. Do not hot-link unvetted media, and do not embed
  anything you have not personally cleared.
- Strip EXIF before publishing. The same GPS and device data that helped you verify can
  deanonymise the person who sent it. Remove it from anything public.
- Caption every published item with provenance and its evidence standing, in the honest
  register style. "Submitted by [handle], [date]. Unverified." is fine and correct.
- Consent: only publish media the submitter agreed to have published. Keep the agreement.

## 5. Content policy (put this on the form and the subreddit)

- Do not submit illegal content of any kind.
- Only submit media you have the right to share.
- Submissions may be reviewed before anything is published.
- No personal data about other people without their consent.

## 6. If illegal material is ever received (read this now, not later)

The realistic worst case is child sexual abuse material (CSAM). Handle it correctly:

- Do NOT download, copy, forward, screenshot, or store it. In UK law, opening or
  downloading such an image can itself count as "making" it. Do not investigate it
  yourself.
- Do NOT reply engaging with the sender.
- Report it promptly:
  - Internet Watch Foundation, the UK hotline for child sexual abuse imagery:
    report.iwf.org.uk
  - The police / National Crime Agency (CEOP for child exploitation), or 999 if a child
    is in immediate danger.
- Record that you reported it, when, and to whom. Keep that note, not the material.
- Prompt, good-faith reporting is the protective path. Get legal advice if you are unsure.

For other illegal material (terrorism, threats, etc.): preserve access details only as
needed, do not spread it, and report to the police, or to gov.uk for terrorism content.

## 7. Online Safety Act, in short

- Illegal-content duties are in force (since 17 March 2025) for user-to-user and search
  services that target UK users, whatever their size or location.
- If your own site does not host user uploads, it is mostly a publisher of your own
  curated content, which keeps it clear of the heaviest user-to-user duties.
- If you ever do host uploads, small and genuinely low-risk services still must complete
  an illegal-content risk assessment and have basic but real measures to remove illegal
  content when they become aware of it. File-upload services get the closest scrutiny.
- The subreddit itself sits on Reddit, so platform-level OSA compliance there is Reddit's
  responsibility, not yours, though you still moderate under Reddit's rules.
- This is a live, evolving area. Check Ofcom's small-service guidance and take legal
  advice before opening uploads or as the project grows.

## 8. Data protection reminder

Media plus a name, a face, a location, or contact details is personal data. Minimise what
you keep, store contact details separately and privately, publish only with consent, strip
EXIF before publishing, and honour deletion requests.
